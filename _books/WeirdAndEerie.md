This is my first Mark Fisher read; all in all, I am delighted and surprised by how approachable Fisher's philosophical, literary, music, and film criticism and analysis is. This is his posthumous work released shortly after his death at the age of 48 in 2017 (I know this is confusing; he died around a month after the UK release, before the worldwide release, so it's kind of his last finished work but inextricably tied to his death with the greater release, so a lot of descriptions describe it as a posthumous release). And I wanted to dig into his writings since I was peripherally inspired by his popularising of the term hauntology - in his case, to make it relevant to "lost futures" from the past which haunt the current time.
