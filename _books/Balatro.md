It's true that Balatro (a Latin term for professional jester) is exceptionally interesting and engaging, deserving of its many awards and recognitions. And it's certainly addictive, between the player feeling clever in their hands and the blessing (or curse) of RNG goddess's.

Comparatively to the traditional roguelike and lite, it's a purely mechanical poker game, with emergent happenstances popping up, but nothing really in the way of narrative outside of personal success and failure in a 5 err... 8 card draw. The mishap and success of system abstracts like rags to riches or the loss of luck, etc.

So what makes it fun? Is it fun? It's a combination of gambling mixed with car collection vernacular, as well as continual adaptability. And that last point, the frankensteining of your deck is akin to the character assembly in standard RPG game structures, where you construct your character, the stats, and the skills. But in this case, it's done via reshaping this monstrous deck into your preferred likeness through various booster Jokers and specialty cards that you're constantly transforming, deleting, adding, and subtracting from a different version of assembly, all at the whim of the resources available. There's something interesting in the Deck assembly, and it's restructuring to adjust the players' strategies toward success. As more and more mechanics come in, starting with the titular jokers, which adjust the overall rules, the deck becomes buffed up from celestial, spectral, and tarot cards, which adjust the makeup of your player deck at large, adding to the rules at an individual card's level.

All in all, it's beyond exceptionally fun! Very creative, and its' closest metaprogression would be the new releases of different types of Jokers and starting deck rules to allow even more amounts of replayability to feel fresh for a long time. There's a little bit of a learning curve, since there are so many different types of boons you can have on an individual card level and on a run or a debt level, from vouchers to spectral cards to planet cards to tarot adapters, but after you get a little bit of a grasp on it, just one more run is even more gripping than Rogue Legacy.

Despite marking it done, I've introduced it to my partner, and I have a feeling we will be playing this game often for a long time to come.
