Cave Story (and in this case, the later rerelease Cave Story+ *) is a beautifully charming, heartbreakingly well-written metroidvania where you play as Quote, a little robot soldier helping out a variety of interesting characters to save little peaceful rabbit critters from the genocide at the hands of a mad doctor wishing to mutate them into a violent slave army.

Mechanically, the game might first appear characteristically vintage in its polish. But that is part of the designed challenge, with a very floaty jump, limited savepoint locations, and small decisions that change the game's ending. But the gameplay is quite engaging with a lot of enjoyable weapon variations and enough hidden secrets and hard - as well as nuanced - bosses to make you feel clever and drive you to try one more attempt rather than throw your controller at the brutal delays and hitboxes (looking at you Spelunky). It's quite an amazing feat that took solo developer Daisuke "Pixel" Amaya five years to make before he released it for free, with cute graphics, banger tracks, and all.

My only particular critique is that there are multiple endings, and to receive the best one, the player has to bypass the game's direction. Instead of feeling clever for exploring everything, you are punished by what feels like being thorough and perceptive. This might not be so harsh if the save file was not singularly overwritten, making the restart of a secondary or even tertiary runs required at this decision just beyond the halfway mark.

This game holds exceptional importance as a stark marking of the viability and excellence of indie (solo developer) games, has been cited hundreds of times for inspiration and started the mainstream excitement around indie game works that so many like Braid (2008), Thomas Was Alone (2012), Super Meat Boy (2010; also, its progenitor - The Mighty Jill Off, also from 2008) and Celeste (2018) would later follow. And it does not disappoint. It's still quite satisfyingly challenging, refreshingly heartfelt, and provides a lot of player agency on how they wish to approach any given challenge. As a platformer and a metriodvania it is definitely an important play worthy of study, particularly in Amaya's non-expositional tutorials utilize excellent design principles of introducing mechanics and threats before ramping up difficulty.

* Cave Story+ was released in 2011 on Steam (updated 2026) and in 2017 for switch. It updates a bit of the graphic fidelity while maintaining the charming and adds those FOMO gambling aspects of achievements, as well as contains some nifty extras such as Curly's story (play as the femme robot deuteragonist) and external game to the solo games' story challenges also al la Spelunky style.
